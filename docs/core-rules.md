# Aphelion 5 — Core Rules (v2 draft)

> SNAPSHOT for programming. Master: the Core Rules doc (https://claude.ai/artifact/9wWDQ9mD2CxCGiDEBEMs3C). Do not edit this file; rebuild it from the doc. Snapshot of 2026-11-04 (master rev 137).

## 1. Introduction

**Aphelion 5** is a solo campaign game. You command a **Company** and its **Flagship**, and you fight your way through a **Campaign** of several missions. Each mission is fought on **the Field**: stacks of face-down tiles that hide enemy units, energy, your own Mecha and dangerous Flares.

### How you win

Complete the objective of every mission in the Campaign, in order. Finish the final mission and you have won.

### How you lose

- Your Flagship is **Crippled** (every Bay is at 0 Integrity) and you have no Overhaul slot left to repair it, **or**
- you have no legal action left, **or**
- you **concede** because you see no reasonable way to complete the mission.

Win or lose, you always score the missions you completed (see 10.3 Game End and scoring).

### The flow of a mission

1. **Mission Preparation** — build the Field and deal the mission deck onto it.
2. **Command phase** — take actions one at a time: reveal tiles, command Mecha, run Modules, use Bays and issue Orders.
3. **Overload** — whenever a Flare is revealed, time slows down. Sentinels and Flares fire, and you react with Overload abilities before everything resolves.
4. Return to the Command phase. Repeat until the mission objective is met.
5. **Mission End** — collect rewards, buy upgrades, and carry what is left into the next mission.

## 2. Components and Iconography

### Boards, sheets and cards

| Component | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign board | Lists the Campaign's missions, its Omega unit stats, its campaign Orders, and the high-score fields (Fresh and Veteran). |
| Mission cards | One per mission: Deployment, Threat columns A–D, objective, Field map and briefing. |

Everything is limited to the counts below. **Damage** tokens, **Alert** tokens and **Wreck markers** are the only unlimited components.

| Token | Count | Short description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Damage | Unlimited | Reduces a unit's Strength by 1. |

### Energy iconography

Card text in this rulebook writes energy out in words. On printed components, use one glyph per colour.

| Written as | Glyph on components | Mostly used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow energy | Yellow glyph | The most expensive Modules |
| Red energy | Red glyph | Overload Modules (red background) |
| Blue energy | Blue glyph | Scanning, mines and reveals |

### Where things live

- **Supply** — the general pool of tokens and energy, and the box of unused tiles.
- **Reserve** — *your* energy, beside your Company sheet. Gained energy goes here unless an effect says to place it on a Module.
- **Upgrade track** — the box on your Company sheet where you write Upgrade points as you gain them.
- **Mission deck** — the face-down stack of tiles you deal onto the Field each mission.
- **Energy deck** — the 42 Energy Gyros, shuffled face down.
- **Discard** — removed tiles, face up.
- **Trophy pile** — collected Power Nodes and destroyed Omega units.

## 3. Core Concepts

### 3.1 The Field and sectors

**The Field** is the battlefield of a mission. You build it from sector plates in the shape of the mission's map. Build it exactly as printed, without rotating it: the shape faces you, so "horizontal" always means left–right from where you sit. Sectors are **pointy-top hexagons**: each has a flat left side and a flat right side, so horizontal rows run straight across the table.

Each plate is a **sector**. Tiles are stacked on top of sector plates. At any moment, a sector is in exactly one of three states:

| State | What you see | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dark** | The top tile is face down. | Unknown. You can Reveal it. It holds no unit. |
| **Occupied** | The top tile is face up. | That top tile is a **unit**. |
| **Cleared** | No tiles at all; the plate is visible. | Nothing left in this sector. |

A **gap** is a space inside the Field's outline where there is no plate. A gap is not a sector.

### 3.2 Tiles and units

Every tile has a face and an identical back. A tile is a **unit** only while it is **face up on top of its sector**. Face-down tiles, and face-up tiles buried under other tiles, are not units: effects cannot see or target them unless the effect says so.

A tile's face shows:

1. The unit's image and glyph.
2. Its **Level** (printed shields). Flares have no Level. An Omega unit's Level is the value on the Campaign board; it counts as that Level for every effect.
3. Its background colour and border, which identify its type (see 4. Units).
4. On some units: energy symbols (Flares, Energy Gyros) and arrows on some sides.

### 3.3 Level and Strength

- **Level** is the number of shields printed on the tile (for Omega units, the value on the Campaign board). It never changes. "A Level 2 unit" means a unit with 2 printed shields.
- **Strength** is how tough the unit is right now:

> **Strength = Level + Shield tokens + Bio-shield tokens − Damage tokens**

Shields and Damage cancel each other out, so a unit never carries both. When you deal damage to a unit with Shields, remove one Shield per point of damage instead of placing Damage. When you Boost a damaged unit, remove one Damage instead of placing a Shield.

When a unit's Strength reaches **0**, it is **destroyed**.

**Values of destroyed units.** Whenever an effect uses a value of a unit that has been destroyed (such as its Strength or its tokens), use the value it had just before it was destroyed.

> **Example:** A Level 2 Bot has 1 Shield token: Strength 3. A Module deals it 2 damage. You remove the Shield, then place 1 Damage token. The Bot is at Strength 1.

### 3.4 When a top tile leaves

Whenever the top unit of a sector leaves it (destroyed, moved, swapped, teleported or buried), look at what is now on top:

- **A face-down tile** — the sector is Dark.
- **A face-up tile** — that tile is **uncovered**. It is now a unit, but it was not *revealed*, so nothing that triggers "when revealed" happens. An uncovered Flare gives no energy and starts no Overload (see 4.6).
- **Nothing** — the sector is Cleared.

### 3.5 Adjacency

Two sectors are **adjacent** if their plates share an edge. Gaps break adjacency. An **adjacent unit** is a unit in an adjacent sector; Dark and Cleared sectors contain no unit.

### 3.6 Rows, lines of fire and arrows

The hex grid has three straight directions. One rule applies to every line on the Field: **a line continues across gaps, Dark sectors and Cleared sectors** until it leaves the Field's outline.

- **Row** — every sector on one straight line through the Field, in any of the three directions.
- **Horizontal row** — a row in the left–right direction.
- **A row through this unit** — card text for: choose one of the three rows that pass through this unit's sector.
- **Arrows** — some units have arrows on their sides. Sides are numbered 1 to 6, starting with the flat left side and going clockwise.
- **Targeted** — the one adjacent sector an arrow points to, and the unit in it. If the arrow points at a gap or off the Field, nothing is Targeted.
- **Line of fire** — every sector in a straight line from an arrow, in the direction it points, to the edge of the Field.

> **Example:** A horizontal row has five sectors, but the third space is a gap. The row is still all four plates on that line, so "deal 1 damage to all units in this horizontal row" hits units on both sides of the gap.

### 3.7 Facing

When you flip a tile face up with a Reveal, or place a unit onto the Field, you choose which way it faces. Units that are moved, swapped, teleported or uncovered keep their facing, unless an effect lets you rotate them.

### 3.8 Geometry (coordinates)

This section states 3.1 and 3.5–3.7 exactly, in coordinates. Mission maps list their sectors this way.

**Coordinates.** Every sector has axial coordinates (q, r). Increasing r by 1 is one step to the right; increasing q by 1 is one step up-right. (0, 0) is each map's starting sector and has no other meaning. A coordinate that is not on the mission map is a gap or off the Field; these rules treat both the same.

**Sides and neighbours.** Sides are numbered 1–6 from the flat left side, clockwise (3.6).

| Side | Direction | Neighbour of (q, r) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | left | (q, r − 1) |
| 2 | upper-left | (q + 1, r − 1) |
| 3 | upper-right | (q + 1, r) |
| 4 | right | (q, r + 1) |
| 5 | lower-right | (q − 1, r + 1) |
| 6 | lower-left | (q − 1, r) |

Opposite sides are 1–4, 2–5 and 3–6. Two sectors are adjacent when one is a neighbour of the other and both are on the map.

**Rows.** Every sector lies on exactly three rows. A row is every map sector that meets its condition, so gaps, Dark and Cleared sectors never break it.

| Row | Sectors in it | Runs along sides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Horizontal row | same q | 1 and 4 |
| Rising diagonal (/) | same r | 3 and 6 |
| Falling diagonal (\\) | same q + r | 2 and 5 |

**Targeted and lines of fire.** For an arrow pointing to side s from sector P, step D is that side's neighbour offset. The Targeted sector is P + D, if it is on the map; otherwise nothing is Targeted. The line of fire is every map sector P + D, P + 2D, P + 3D and so on: it skips gaps instead of stopping at them, and never includes P.

**Facing.** Every face-up tile has a facing f from 0 to 5; f = 0 is as printed. A printed arrow on side s points to side ((s − 1 + f) mod 6) + 1. Turning clockwise by one side adds 1 to f (6 becomes 0). Reveal and Place set f; every other way a tile changes place keeps it (3.7).

**Straight lines.** "A sector in a straight line from a unit" is any map sector P + kD, for any of the six steps D and any k of 1 or more.

> **Example (Mission 1):** The map draws as three horizontal rows. Through (0, 0) run the horizontal row (0, −1) (0, 0) (0, 1) (0, 2), the rising diagonal (−1, 0) (0, 0) (1, 0) and the falling diagonal (−1, 1) (0, 0) (1, −1).

```
        (1,-1)   (1,0)    (1,1)
   (0,-1)   (0,0)    (0,1)    (0,2)
        (-1,0)   (-1,1)   (-1,2)
```

> **Example (a row across a gap, Mission 2x):** (0, 2) is a gap. The rising diagonal r = 2 is (1, 2) and (−1, 2): one row, so a row effect from (1, 2) also hits (−1, 2). The two sectors are not adjacent.

## 4. Units

There are seven unit types. You can tell them apart by background colour; the border tells you where the tile goes at Mission End.

| Type | Background | Border | When it acts | When removed, goes to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bot | White | None | Never | Discard |
| Mecha | Orange | None | When you Command it | Discard |
| Gyro | Green | Dark (Energy Gyros: dark/yellow stripes) | When you Drain it, or when it is destroyed | Discard |
| Sleeper | Blue | Dark | When no Dark sectors remain | Discard |
| Sentinel | Red | Dark | At the start of every Overload | Discard |
| Flare | Yellow | Dark (Power Node: white/black stripes) | When revealed | Discard at Overload End (Power Node: Trophy pile) |
| Omega | Violet | White/black stripes | As listed on the Campaign board | Trophy pile |

### 4.1 Bots

Bots are obstacles. They have no effects. They are your Mecha's natural target, and many effects count them.

### 4.2 Mecha

Mecha are your own units, hidden in the mission deck among the enemy. Once revealed, you can **Command** them in the Command phase (see 8.4). A Mecha's abilities are printed on your Company sheet. Every Command, choose one:

- **Attack** — Choose an adjacent unit. Deal it damage equal to this Mecha's Strength, as a single hit. Then destroy this Mecha. The Mecha is destroyed even if its target had less Strength.
- **Scrap** — Gain energy equal to this Mecha's Strength, in any mix of colours. Then destroy this Mecha.
- **Use an ability** — Use one ability listed for this Mecha on your Company sheet.

You can Command the same Mecha any number of times, one action each time, as long as it survives.

> **Example:** Your Level 3 M\_04 has 1 Damage token, so its Strength is 2. You Attack a Level 3 Bot two sectors away in a straight line (M\_04's Passive lets it). The Bot takes 2 damage and drops to Strength 1. Your M\_04 is destroyed.

**Ability tags.** Every ability on a Company sheet starts with a tag that says when and how you use it:

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Command:** | Optional. Use it when you Command this Mecha in the Command phase. |
| **Expend:** | As Command, then destroy this Mecha. |
| **Passive:** | Always on. Modifies how the Mecha moves or attacks. Not an action by itself. |
| **\[Overload\]** | Placed before Command or Expend. You may use it only during an Overload, never in the Command phase. |
| **At Overload:** | Mandatory trigger. This unit gets an Alert token at the start of every Overload, and you must resolve it. |

The same tags are used on Bays and Orders.

**When an effect alerts a Mecha** (for example, "Activate all Mecha"), the Mecha gets an Alert token. When you resolve it, you must Command that Mecha: Attack, Scrap, or one of its abilities, ignoring timing tags. This also applies during an Overload: an alerted Mecha may Attack or Scrap there (see 9.2).

**Allied Mecha.** Mecha from Allied Drops (see 7.3) stay with you for the rest of the campaign: at Mission End they go into your mission deck with your own Mecha (10.1). Once mixed in, you cannot tell them apart. Each Mecha uses one sheet:

- If your Company lists that Mecha, it uses your sheet.
- Otherwise, it uses your Ally's sheet (the Company named on your sheet; see 7.3).

### 4.3 Gyros

Gyros carry two effects: a **Drain** effect and a **Destroyed** effect.

- **Drain** — In the Command phase, you may Drain a Gyro as an action: resolve its Drain effect, then discard it.
- **Destroyed** — When a Gyro is destroyed, it becomes a **Wreck** (see 4.8) with an Alert token. When the Alert resolves, resolve its Destroyed effect, then discard it.

**Energy Gyros** (G\_01–G\_09, 42 tiles) are the Gyros with dark/yellow striped borders. Their effects give energy. Together they form the **Energy deck**. Wherever a Deployment lists **G\_00**, take one tile from the top of the Energy deck without looking at it.

### 4.4 Sleepers

Sleepers lie dormant until the Field is fully exposed.

**Trigger:** whenever there are **no Dark sectors** on the Field, every Sleeper unit without an Alert token gets one. This is a constant condition, not a one-time check:

- It does not matter how the last Dark sector disappeared: a Reveal, or a unit moved, placed or teleported on top of it.
- Sleepers that become units while the condition is true (for example, an uncovered Sleeper) are alerted at once.
- Several Sleepers can trigger at the same moment. You choose the order in which to resolve them.
- A unit never holds more than one Alert token, so a waiting Sleeper is not alerted twice.

After its effect resolves, discard the Sleeper.

> **Example:** Two sectors are Dark. You Reveal one: a Bot. One Dark sector is left. You Command your M\_01 to move from a sector that would become Cleared into the last Dark sector. Now no Dark sectors remain: every Sleeper on the Field is alerted, without you revealing anything.

### 4.5 Sentinels

Sentinels are the enemy's defenders. They act only during Overloads: at the start of every Overload, every Sentinel on the Field gets an Alert token (see 9.1). A Sentinel revealed during an Overload already under way does not act in that Overload. Every Sentinel that is revealed gets a Barrier token at once, as part of the Reveal and before anything else happens (see 6.4). An uncovered Sentinel, or one put onto the Field any other way, gets none.

### 4.6 Flares

Flares are energy surges that set off Overloads.

- **No Level, no Strength, never chosen.** Flares cannot be damaged or destroyed, and no effect can choose a Flare. A Flare never gains or loses tokens, except its own Alert token. Effects that hit or Boost "all" units skip Flares; effects that count units still count them.
- **When revealed**, a Flare fires:
  1. Take the energy shown on the Flare from the Supply and place it on ready Module(s) of the same colour (see 5.3). A **Power Node** gives one Yellow, one Red and one Blue energy; each goes on a ready Module of its colour.
  2. If it is the Command phase, start an Overload once the current effect has finished (see 9.1). If an Overload is already under way, give the Flare an Alert token instead.
- **Its effect** (printed on the tile) resolves when its Alert token resolves during the Overload. A Power Node has no effect.
- **At Overload End**, remove every Flare unit: Flares go to the Discard, Power Nodes go to the Trophy pile.
- **Uncovered Flares** were never revealed, so they give no energy and start no Overload. Otherwise they are ordinary Flares: the next Overload alerts them, and Overload End removes them (Power Nodes go to the Trophy pile).

### 4.7 Omega units

Omega units (Alpha, Beta, Gamma) are each Campaign's main enemies. Their Level and effects are printed on the Campaign board, not on the tile. An Omega unit's Level is the value on the Campaign board, and it counts as that Level for every effect. Most Omega units are placed face up on the Field during Mission Preparation; some are shuffled into the mission deck.

- When an Omega unit is destroyed, it goes to the **Trophy pile** (after resolving its Destroyed effect, if it has one).
- It counts toward an Eliminate objective however it was destroyed.
- Omega units never take Exposure damage (see 9.3).

> **Example:** During an Overload, a Beta resolves "At Overload: destroy one Alpha unit." The Alpha becomes a Wreck, resolves its Destroyed effect, and goes to the Trophy pile. If your objective is "Eliminate 6 Alpha units", that Alpha counts.

### 4.8 Wrecks

A **Wreck** is a destroyed unit waiting for its Destroyed effect to resolve. When a unit with a Destroyed effect is destroyed:

1. Return all its tokens to the Supply.
2. Place an Alert token and a **Wreck marker** on it.
3. It stays in its sector until its Alert resolves. Then resolve its Destroyed effect, return the Wreck marker, and remove the unit: an Omega unit goes to the Trophy pile, every other unit to the Discard.

While it waits, a Wreck **is still a unit**: it counts for Anomalies and for effects that count units, it occupies its sector, and it blocks movement. But a Wreck has no Strength, **no effect can choose it**, and it never gains or loses tokens other than its Alert, which it loses only by resolving. Effects that hit or Boost "all" units skip it. It cannot be moved, swapped, buried or flipped.

> **Example:** An Alpha is destroyed. Its Destroyed effect says "Boost one adjacent non-Mecha unit." The only adjacent units are a Flare and another Wreck. Neither can be chosen, so the Boost has no legal target and does nothing.

## 5. Your Company

### 5.1 The Company sheet

At the start of the game you choose one Company. Its sheet lists:

- **Name**
- **Flagship** — its Bays, their Integrity and their abilities
- **Starting energy** — taken from the Supply into your Reserve at setup
- **Overhaul slots** — how many times you can rebuild your Flagship
- **Upgrade track** — where you write Upgrade points as you earn them
- **Starting Mecha** — which Mecha tiles go into your mission deck, and their abilities
- **Upgrades** — Mecha upgrades, Flagship upgrades and Requisitions (see 10.2)

The sheet is written on with an erasable marker. Its upgrades persist between games (see Fresh and Veteran campaigns, 7.1).

**Company Trait.** Some Companies have a Trait: a special rule printed on the sheet that is always in effect. A Trait says when it applies.

> **Example:** The Support Vessel of Ko and Van has the Trait "At Mission End: remove 1 damage from each Bay of your Flagship."

### 5.2 The Flagship and its Bays

Your Flagship is made of several **Bays**. Each Bay has an **Integrity** value and one or more abilities.

- **Using a Bay** costs 1 Integrity from that Bay. A Bay at 0 Integrity cannot be used.
- **Damage to your Flagship:** assign each point to any Bay that still has Integrity. You may split it between Bays.
- **Repairing your Flagship** ("remove damage from your Flagship"): restore that much Integrity, split between Bays as you like, never above each Bay's printed value.
- Damage to your Flagship carries over from one mission to the next, unless a Company Trait repairs it.

**Crippled.** Your Flagship is Crippled while every Bay is at 0 Integrity. Being Crippled does not end the game by itself:

- Further damage to a Crippled Flagship is ignored.
- During an Overload, play on normally. You cannot use Bays, but effects that repair your Flagship (such as an Anomaly reward) can still save it.
- Before each Command phase action, check your Flagship. If it is Crippled, your next action must be an **Overhaul** (see 8.8). If you have no empty Overhaul slot, you lose.

> **Example:** During an Overload, a Sentinel deals your last 2 Integrity of damage: your Flagship is Crippled. You keep resolving the Overload. At Overload End you complete an Anomaly and take "Remove 3 damage from your Flagship". When the Command phase begins, your Flagship is no longer Crippled, so you do not need to Overhaul.

### 5.3 Modules

Modules are your weapons and ship systems. Each Module card shows a name, an image, an energy cost and colour, an effect, a background colour and an ID.

- **Ready and exhausted.** A ready Module is face up. After you run it, turn it face down: it is exhausted. Exhausted Modules become ready again at Mission End, so each Module can usually be run once per mission.
- **Background colour.** Light-background Modules can be run only in the Command phase. Red-background Modules can be run only during an Overload.
- **Free runs follow the same rule.** An effect that lets you run a Module without paying its cost still uses the background rule: a light Module in the Command phase, a red one during an Overload. A Module with an X cost run for free uses X = 1.
- **Energy on Modules.** Some effects place energy directly on Modules. Energy can only be placed on ready Modules; if you have none, the energy stays in the Supply. When an effect lets you choose the Module or the colour, the energy must match the Module's colour; if no ready Module of that colour exists, the energy stays in the Supply. An effect that names both the Module and the colour places them as written. Energy on a Module can only help pay for that Module, and only for costs of its own colour.
- You may return energy from any Module to the Supply at any time.
- Your Modules carry over from mission to mission. There is no hand limit.

The step-by-step procedure for running a Module is in 8.6.

### 5.4 Energy

There are three energy colours: Yellow, Red and Blue, 10 of each in the Supply.

- Gained energy goes to your **Reserve**, unless the effect says to place it on a Module.
- If an effect does not name a colour, you choose (you may mix colours).
- When you lose energy, you choose which colours to lose. If you do not have enough, lose all you have.
- Energy in your Reserve carries over from mission to mission. Energy on Modules does not.

### 5.5 Anomaly cards

Anomaly cards describe battlefield conditions, such as "At least 4 Mecha on the Field". You check them at Overload End (see 9.3). Requirements are checked at that moment only; a Wreck counts as a unit.

Anomaly cards carry over from mission to mission. There is no hand limit.

### 5.6 Orders and Command tokens

**Orders** are special actions printed in a mission's briefing and on the Campaign board. Each Order shows its cost in Command tokens, then its effect:

> **2 Command tokens:** Destroy one Level 2 Bot.

- You take 6 Command tokens at the start of each mission and return them all at Mission End. You can never hold more than 6.
- You can issue the same Order any number of times, as long as you can pay for it.
- An Order tagged **\[Overload\]** can be issued only during an Overload; all other Orders only in the Command phase.

## 6. Tokens

Tokens sit on units and move with them. When a unit leaves the Field, return its tokens to the Supply unless a rule says otherwise.

### 6.1 Damage and hits

Each Damage token lowers a unit's Strength by 1 (see 3.3).

A **hit** is one instruction that deals damage to one unit, whatever the amount. "Deal 3 damage to a unit" is one hit of 3. "Deal 1 damage to all units in a row" is one hit of 1 on each of those units. "Deal damage" with no number means 1 damage.

### 6.2 Shield tokens (Boost)

To **Boost** a unit, place one Shield token on it. Each Shield adds 1 Strength. Shields do not change a unit's Level.

There are only 10 Shield tokens. If you must Boost and no Shield token is left, deal 1 damage to your Flagship instead, once for each missing Shield.

Flares and Wrecks cannot be Boosted. Effects that Boost "all" units skip them; this does not damage your Flagship.

### 6.3 Bio-shield tokens

A Bio-shield is a Shield token in every way: it adds 1 Strength and cancels Damage exactly like a Shield. It also has two effects of its own:

- **Drain** — In the Command phase, as an action, remove a Bio-shield from a unit and Boost one non-Mecha unit adjacent to that unit.
- **Destroyed** — When damage removes a Bio-shield, deal 1 damage to one unit adjacent to the unit it was on, of the same type as that unit. Resolve this as soon as the effect that removed it has finished.

When a unit with both Shields and Bio-shields takes damage, you choose which tokens are removed first. A Bio-shield that cancels a Damage token when it is placed is simply returned to the Supply, with no effect.

> **Example:** A Level 1 Gyro carries a Bio-shield, so its Strength is 2. It takes 1 damage, which removes the Bio-shield. When that effect finishes, the Bio-shield deals 1 damage to an adjacent Gyro.

### 6.4 Barrier tokens

A Barrier cancels the **next hit** on its unit completely, whatever the amount. Then remove the Barrier.

- A unit can hold only one Barrier. Every revealed Sentinel starts with one (see 4.5); if no Barrier is left in the Supply, it gets none (see 6.7).
- A Barrier does not stop Destroy effects, which do not deal damage.

> **Example:** Your Strength 3 Mecha Attacks a Sentinel with a Barrier. The whole 3-damage hit is cancelled and the Barrier is removed. Your Mecha is still destroyed, because Attacking always destroys the attacker.

### 6.5 Alert tokens

An Alert token marks a unit whose effect has been triggered and is waiting to resolve. When you resolve it, remove the token and carry out that unit's effect.

- A unit can hold only one Alert token.
- In the Command phase, while any Alert token is on the Field, your only allowed actions are Resolve Alerts and Overhaul (see 8.1).
- If an Alert token is removed without being resolved (for example, by Bury), that effect is cancelled.
- Effects that remove Alert tokens say which units they work on (for example, "remove the Alert token from one Sentinel"). They never work on Flares or Wrecks.

### 6.6 Fuse tokens

Fuse tokens are placed on units. They go off in step 1 of Overload End (see 9.3), one at a time, in the order you choose. After a token goes off, remove it.

| Fuse token | When it goes off |
| --- | --- |
| Tick | Deals 2 damage to its unit. |
| Mortar | Deals 1 damage to one unit adjacent to its unit (your choice). |
| Feedback | Deals 1 damage to your Flagship. |

If the unit carrying a Fuse token leaves the Field or becomes a Wreck before that token goes off, the token returns to the Supply without going off.

> **Example:** During the Overload End step 1. A Gyro carries a Tick and a Mortar. You set off the Tick first: its 2 damage destroys the Gyro, which becomes a Wreck. The Mortar returns to the Supply without firing. If you want the Mortar's damage, set it off before the Tick.

### 6.7 Running out

If an effect needs more tokens, energy or tiles than are available, use what there is and ignore the rest. The only exception is Boost (see 6.2).

## 7. Setup and Mission Preparation

### 7.1 Game setup

1. **Choose a Campaign** and take its Campaign board.
2. **Choose a Company** and take its sheet.
3. **Fresh or Veteran.** If the sheet has no marked upgrade slots, you play a **Fresh** campaign. If it has marks from earlier games, choose:
   - **Fresh** — erase every mark on the sheet, or
   - **Veteran** — keep all upgrade marks.

   Mark your choice on the Campaign board. In both cases, erase any marked Overhaul slots: you always start with all of them.
4. **Take your starting energy** from the Supply into your Reserve, as listed on your sheet.
5. **Build the mission deck:** all 30 Bots (10 each of Level 1, 2 and 3) plus the starting Mecha listed on your sheet.
6. **Build the Energy deck:** shuffle all 42 Energy Gyros face down.
7. **Shuffle the Anomaly deck** and take 3 cards.
8. **Take your starting Modules.** Shuffle the Module deck. Reveal Modules one at a time and keep the first two of each energy colour (6 in total). Put every other revealed Module on the bottom of the deck.
9. Go to Mission Preparation.

### 7.2 Mission Preparation

Do these steps at the start of every mission.

1. **Choose the mission.** Play the lowest-numbered mission you have not completed. Some numbers have alternative routes (for example 2x and 2y): choose one. The other route is skipped for this campaign.
2. **Deploy.** Add the tiles in the mission's Deployment to the mission deck. "6x G\_00" means six tiles from the top of the Energy deck, unseen. An entry with no copy count (such as "O\_02") is not shuffled in: place one such tile face up on each sector the map marks for it.
3. **Choose the Threat Level.** Choose how many Threat columns (A–D) to play, then which ones, and mark them on the Campaign board. Add the four tiles listed under each chosen column to the mission deck.

   | Threat columns | Threat Level |
   | --- | --- |
   | 1 | Patrol |
   | 2 | Assault |
   | 3 | Siege |
   | 4 | Annihilation |

   You may choose a different Threat Level and different columns in every mission. Each column you complete scores 1 point at Game End.
4. **Build the Field** from sector plates, exactly in the shape of the mission map and without rotating it.
5. **Deal the mission deck.** Shuffle it and deal it face down, as evenly as possible, onto every sector that does not already hold an Omega unit. If the tiles do not divide evenly, you choose which sectors receive one extra tile.
6. **Draw a Module.** Reveal the top 3 Modules. Keep 1 and put the other 2 on the bottom of the deck, in random order.
7. **Take 6 Command tokens.**
8. **Draw 1 Anomaly card.**
9. **Resolve the mission event** printed in the briefing, if any (for example, an Allied Drop).

The Command phase begins.

> **Example (dealing):** Mission 3's map has 12 sectors, and 3 of them hold a Beta. Your mission deck has 84 tiles. You deal 9 tiles to each of the other 9 sectors (81 tiles). You then choose 3 of those sectors to receive a 10th tile.

> **Tip:** Each mission card lists every unit used in that mission. If you have table space, take those units out of the box and set them nearby before you start.

### 7.3 Allied Drop

An Allied Drop brings in a Mecha from another Company. The briefing gives a Level, for example "Allied Drop — Level 2".

1. **Choose your Ally.** At your first Allied Drop of the campaign, choose one Company other than your own and write its name on your Company sheet. Every Allied Drop in this campaign comes from that Company.
2. **Choose a Mecha.** Choose one Mecha your Ally lists, of that Level or lower, and take it from the Supply. It may be the same Mecha as one of your own. If no copy of it is left in the Supply, you cannot choose it.
3. **Place it** face up on top of any Dark sector, facing any direction.

An Allied Mecha uses your Ally's sheet, including upgrades filled on it in earlier games. If your Company lists the same Mecha, it uses your sheet instead (see 4.2). It stays in your mission deck for the rest of the campaign.

## 8. The Command Phase

### 8.1 Taking actions

In the Command phase you take actions one at a time, as many as you like, in any order. **Before each action**, check in this order:

1. **Objective** — Is the mission objective met? If so, the mission is won: go straight to Mission End (see 8.2).
2. **Flagship** — Is your Flagship Crippled? If so, you must Overhaul now. If you cannot, you lose.

Then choose one action:

| Action | Section |
| --- | --- |
| Resolve Alerts | 8.3 |
| Command a Mecha | 8.4 |
| Reveal | 8.5 |
| Run a Module | 8.6 |
| Use a Bay | 8.7 |
| Drain a Gyro or Bio-shield | 8.7 |
| Issue an Order | 8.7 |
| Overhaul | 8.8 |

**Alerts first.** While any Alert token is on the Field, you may only Resolve Alerts or Overhaul.

An action is legal if you can start it: you can pay its cost and meet its conditions (for example, a ready Module, a Bay with Integrity, an empty Overhaul slot). It is legal even if its effect would do nothing (11.1, rule 5). If you have no legal action left, you lose.

### 8.2 Mission objectives

Each mission has one objective. A mission is **Salvage** unless its card names an Omega type and number; then it is **Eliminate**.

- **Salvage** — at least **5 Power Nodes** in your Trophy pile **and** at least **3 Cleared sectors** on the Field.
- **Eliminate** — at least the listed number of the named Omega units in your Trophy pile (for example, "Eliminate 6 Alpha units").

**When the objective is met.** You check the objective only before Command phase actions, so an Overload already under way always finishes first. Once the objective is met, the mission is won: go straight to Mission End. Any Alert tokens still on the Field are simply cleared. If your Flagship is Crippled at that moment, the mission still counts. Your Flagship is next checked before your first action of the next mission (8.1), after any Mission End repairs.

### 8.3 Resolve Alerts

Remove Alert tokens one at a time and resolve each unit's effect, in any order you choose, until none are left. This includes Alerts created along the way.

If one of these effects starts an Overload, the Overload begins as soon as that effect finishes. Any Alert tokens still waiting carry into the Overload and are resolved there.

### 8.4 Command a Mecha

Choose one of your Mecha units and Attack, Scrap or use one of its abilities without the \[Overload\] tag (see 4.2).

### 8.5 Reveal

Choose a Dark sector. Flip its top tile face up and choose its facing. That tile is now a unit.

- If it is a Sentinel, place a Barrier on it (see 4.5). If it is a Flare, it fires (see 4.6).
- If this leaves no Dark sectors on the Field, every Sleeper is alerted (see 4.4).
- Card text "just revealed" means revealed during the effect or action that is resolving right now.

### 8.6 Run a Module

Choose a ready Module with a light background.

1. **Use stored energy.** You may set aside any of the energy on this Module to help pay for it.
2. **Pay.** Pay the rest of the cost from your Reserve, in the Module's colour. Discounts printed on the Module apply; a cost never drops below 0.
3. **Resolve** its effect. If the effect offers an extra payment for a bigger effect, you may pay it or skip that part.
4. **Exhaust** the Module: turn it face down.
5. **Clean up.** Any energy still on this Module, or set aside and not spent, returns to the Supply.

The Module being run is not ready while it resolves: energy cannot be placed on it.

> **Example:** *Accumulator Beam* has 4 Yellow energy on it. Its cost is 1 Yellow; you pay it with one of those 4. It deals damage equal to the Yellow energy on it before you ran it: 4. The 3 unspent Yellow return to the Supply.

### 8.7 Use a Bay, Drain, Issue an Order

- **Use a Bay** — Choose a Bay with at least 1 Integrity and one of its abilities without the \[Overload\] tag. Deal 1 damage to that Bay, then resolve the ability.
- **Drain a Gyro** — Resolve a Gyro's Drain effect, then discard it (see 4.3).
- **Drain a Bio-shield** — Remove a Bio-shield and Boost an adjacent non-Mecha unit (see 6.3).
- **Issue an Order** — Pay the Command tokens for an Order without the \[Overload\] tag and resolve it (see 5.6).

### 8.8 Overhaul

An Overhaul rebuilds your Flagship. You must Overhaul when your Flagship is Crippled; you may Overhaul at any other time in the Command phase. Either way, you need an empty Overhaul slot.

1. Mark one empty Overhaul slot on your sheet.
2. Remove all damage from your Flagship.
3. Refill your Reserve: for each colour, take energy from the Supply until you have at least your starting amount of that colour.
4. Put one of your Modules on the bottom of the Module deck, then take the top two Modules. They are ready.
5. You may choose up to 2 units (not Flares or Wrecks). Remove all their tokens (including Alert tokens) and put each one at the bottom of its own sector, face up.

## 9. Overloads

An Overload is a slow-motion scene. Everything is happening at once, but you decide the order. You can strike a Sentinel before its effect goes off, or let a Flare reveal more of the Field first.

### 9.1 Starting an Overload

An Overload starts when, during the Command phase, a Flare is revealed or an effect says to start an Overload. It begins **after the current effect has finished**.

> **Example:** *Satellite Ion Scan* reveals units one by one. The second unit it reveals is a Flare: place the Flare's energy right away, but keep revealing. The Overload starts only when the Scan is finished.

When an Overload starts:

1. Place an Alert token on every Flare and every Sentinel on the Field.
2. Place an Alert token on every unit with an **At Overload:** trigger (such as some Omega units and Mecha).
3. The Overload begins.

**Overloads never stack.** During an Overload or Overload End, effects that would start an Overload do nothing.

### 9.2 During an Overload

Take these actions in any order, as many times as you like:

- **Resolve one Alert token** of your choice.
- **Run a Module** with a red background (same steps as 8.6).
- **Command a Mecha** to use an **\[Overload\]** ability.
- **Use a Bay**'s **\[Overload\]** ability.
- **Issue an \[Overload\] Order.**

You cannot take Attack, Scrap, Reveal, Drain or Overhaul as actions during an Overload. This limits only the actions you choose, not effects: when a unit, Module, Bay or Order effect tells you to Reveal, Drain or Command, do it, even during an Overload. For example, F\_04 reveals a unit, and F\_13 alerts every Mecha. When you resolve an alerted Mecha's Alert token during an Overload, it may Attack or Scrap (see 4.2).

During an Overload, these units get an Alert token as soon as it happens: a newly revealed Flare, a unit that becomes a Wreck, Sleepers when the last Dark sector disappears, and units an effect tells you to activate. Sentinels and At Overload units that appear after the Overload started are not alerted.

If a unit with an Alert token is destroyed before you resolve it, its Alert effect never happens (unless it has a Destroyed effect, which then replaces it; see 4.8).

**Ending the Overload.** Once no Alert tokens remain on the Field, you may end the Overload. You may still take more Overload actions first.

> **Example:** An Overload starts. S\_01 ("Deal 1 damage to your Flagship") and the Flare F\_04 are alerted. S\_01 lost its Barrier to an earlier hit, so it has none now. You run the red Module *Charged Beam* on S\_01 and destroy it, so it never damages you. Then you resolve F\_04: you reveal a Level 2 Bot and deal 2 damage to it. No Alerts remain, so you end the Overload.

### 9.3 Overload End

Do these steps in order:

1. **Fuse tokens.** Every Fuse token on the Field goes off, in the order you choose (see 6.6).
2. **Exposure.** Every unit standing alone on its sector plate, with no tiles beneath it, takes 1 damage. Omega units are immune. Flares and Wrecks ignore it as usual.
3. **Anomaly.** If any of your Anomaly cards' requirements are met right now, you may choose **one** of those cards, or none (then nothing happens and every card stays in your hand). Put the chosen card on the bottom of the Anomaly deck and choose one reward:
   - Remove 3 damage from your Flagship, **or**
   - take 6 energy in any mix of colours, **or**
   - gain 6 Upgrade points.
4. **Clear Flares.** Remove every Flare unit from the Field. Flares go to the Discard; Power Nodes go to your Trophy pile.
5. **Return to the Command phase.** Any Alert tokens created during these steps (for example, a Gyro destroyed by a Tick) are resolved there with Resolve Alerts.

## 10. Mission End, Upgrades and Game End

### 10.1 Mission End

Once the objective is met (see 8.2), apply your Company Trait if it acts at Mission End. Then do these steps in order:

1. **Power Node rewards.** Gain 1 Upgrade point for each Power Node in your Trophy pile. Write them on your Upgrade track.
2. **Upgrade.** Spend Upgrade points to mark upgrade slots (see 10.2). Then erase any unspent points: they are lost.
3. **Clear the Trophy pile.** Return its tiles to the Supply.
4. **Sort the Discard** by border:
   - **No border** (Bots and Mecha, including Allied Mecha): add them to the mission deck.
   - **Dark/yellow stripes** (Energy Gyros): put them on the bottom of the Energy deck, face down, in random order.
   - **Everything else**: return it to the Supply.
5. **Clear tokens.** Return every token on the Field to the Supply.
6. **Collect the Field.** Flip every tile on the Field face down, without looking, and add them all to the mission deck. Every unit still on the Field, of any type, carries over this way.
7. **Return all Command tokens** to the Supply.
8. **Reset Modules.** Return all energy on Modules to the Supply and make every Module ready.
9. **Continue.** Go to Mission Preparation for the next mission. If this was the final mission, go to Game End.

| Carries over to the next mission | Does not carry over |
| --- | --- |
| Every tile still on the Field (shuffled into the mission deck) | Discarded Sentinels, Gyros, Sleepers and Flares |
| Discarded Bots and Mecha | The Trophy pile |
| Damage on your Flagship and marked Overhaul slots | Tokens on units |
| Energy in your Reserve | Energy on Modules |
| Modules and Anomaly cards | Command tokens |
| Upgrades | Unspent Upgrade points |

> **Example (copy limits):** There are 4 copies of S\_12. In Mission 1 you play Threat columns A and C, which add all four. All four are still on the Field when you win, so they go into your mission deck. In Mission 3, every Threat column lists an S\_12, but none is left in the Supply, so that entry adds nothing (see 6.7). The column still scores its full point. This is intended: threats you leave alive come back later, but they also use up copies that later Threat columns would have added.

### 10.2 Upgrades

Every Company sheet has its own upgrades. Each has a row of slots; 1 Upgrade point marks 1 slot.

- **When you gain points.** Write them on your Upgrade track as you earn them (Power Nodes, Anomaly rewards). You spend them only at Mission End.
- **Partial progress is kept.** You may mark some of an upgrade's slots now and finish it in a later mission.
- **Fully filled.** When every slot of an upgrade is marked, you gain it permanently.
- **New options, not replacements.** An upgraded Mecha or Bay ability is a new option. Whenever you use that Mecha or Bay, choose any one of its abilities: the original or any upgraded version. An upgrade that improves another ability simply applies to it.
- **Locked upgrades** cannot be marked until the upgrade that unlocks them is fully filled. You may fill both during the same Mission End.
- **Exclusive upgrades.** Some upgrades exclude each other. Once you mark a slot on one, the others can never be marked unless the sheet is erased for a Fresh campaign.
- **Requisitions** are one-time rewards. When a Requisition is fully filled, gain its effect at once. If it is marked **Repeatable**, erase its slots so you can fill it again.

> **Example:** Kew'n X2's Bay 1 starts with "Deal 1 damage to a Level 3 unit". You fill its upgrade "Deal 1 damage to a Level 2 unit". From now on, each time you use Bay 1, you choose to damage either a Level 3 or a Level 2 unit.

### 10.3 Game End and scoring

The game ends when you complete the final mission, when you lose, or when you concede. You may concede at any time; it counts as a loss. In every case, score:

- **+1 point** for each Threat column of every mission you completed.
- **+5 points** if you completed the whole campaign.
- **Modifiers** for optional modules (for example, the Heat module subtracts 2).

**High scores.** The Campaign board has two high-score fields: a plain one for Fresh campaigns and a starred one for Veteran campaigns. If your score equals or beats the score in the matching field, write in your score with your name or the date.

**Clean up.** Erase every mark on the Campaign board except the high scores. On your Company sheet, erase the Upgrade track, the Overhaul slots and your Ally's name, but keep the upgrade marks: they power future Veteran campaigns and Allied Drops.

## 11. Timing, Golden Rules and Glossary

### 11.1 Golden rules

1. **Component text beats the rulebook.** If text on a component contradicts these rules, follow the component.
2. **You make every choice.** Whenever the game offers a choice, including breaking a tie ("the most abundant Level"), you decide. Use it to your advantage.
3. **Finish what you start.** Fully resolve the current action or effect before anything it triggers. Triggered units get an Alert token and wait their turn. An effect finishes even if its own unit is destroyed partway through.
4. **Simultaneous triggers.** When several units trigger at the same moment, you choose the order in which to resolve them.
5. **Do as much as you can.** If part of an effect is impossible, do the rest and ignore that part. If told to reveal 3 units when only 1 Dark sector is left, reveal 1.
6. **Effects that activate other units** ("Drain all Gyros", "Activate all Sleepers", "Activate all Mecha") give each of those units an Alert token. When you resolve it:
   - a Gyro resolves its Drain effect and is discarded;
   - a Sleeper resolves its effect and is discarded;
   - a Mecha must be Commanded (see 4.2).

   If an alerted Gyro is destroyed before it drains, it becomes a Wreck: its Destroyed effect replaces the Drain.

> **Example (your choice):** P\_04 resolves: "Deal 1 damage to all units of the most abundant Level." The Field has three Level 1 units (P\_04 itself, which counts while it resolves, and two of your M\_01s) and three Level 2 Bots. It is a tie, so you choose Level 2. Your Mecha are spared and the Bots take the damage.

### 11.2 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Activate** | An effect that activates a unit gives it an Alert token; the unit's effect resolves later (11.1, rule 6). |
| **Adjacent** | Sharing an edge. Gaps break adjacency (3.5, 3.8). |
| **Alert token** | Marks an effect waiting to resolve. One per unit (6.5). |
| **At Overload:** | Mandatory trigger: alerted at the start of every Overload (4.2). |
| **Barrier** | Cancels the next hit on its unit, whatever the amount (6.4). |
| **Boost** | Place 1 Shield token on a unit (6.2). |
| **Bury** | Remove all tokens from the unit, including Alert tokens, then swap it with the tile directly beneath it, keeping both tiles' faces as they are. If nothing is beneath it, Bury does nothing. Then check the new top tile (3.4). |
| **Cleared sector** | A sector with no tiles (3.1). |
| **Command** | Use one of your Mecha: Attack, Scrap or an ability (4.2). |
| **Crippled** | Every Bay of your Flagship is at 0 Integrity (5.2). |
| **Dark sector** | A sector whose top tile is face down (3.1). Any effect that lets a unit go to, or be placed on, a Dark sector may use a Cleared sector instead. |
| **Destroy** | Remove a unit whatever its Strength. This is not damage: Barriers do not stop it, and Destroyed effects still trigger. |
| **Discard** | The face-up pile of removed tiles. "Discard a tile" means put it there; it is sorted at Mission End (10.1). |
| **Drain** | Resolve a Gyro's or Bio-shield's Drain effect (4.3, 6.3). |
| **Exhausted** | A face-down Module. It cannot be run until it is ready again (5.3). |
| **Expend** | Use this ability, then destroy this Mecha. |
| **Exposure** | 1 damage at Overload End to each unit alone on its sector plate (9.3). |
| **Flip** | Turn a tile over. Flipping face up is only a Reveal when the text says Reveal. A unit flipped face down stops being a unit, and its sector becomes Dark. |
| **Hit** | One instruction dealing damage to one unit, whatever the amount (6.1). |
| **Horizontal row** | A row in the left–right direction (3.6, 3.8). |
| **Just revealed** | Revealed during the action or effect resolving right now. |
| **Level** | Printed shields on a tile; for Omega units, the value on the Campaign board. Never changes (3.2, 3.3). |
| **Line of fire** | Every sector in a straight line from an arrow to the edge of the Field (3.6, 3.8). |
| **Move** | The top unit goes to an **adjacent** Dark or Cleared sector, with its tokens. If it enters a Dark sector, it sits on top of that stack. |
| **Occupied sector** | A sector whose top tile is face up; that tile is a unit (3.1). |
| **\[Overload\]** | Ability, Module or Order usable only during an Overload. |
| **Place** | Put a unit, tile, token or energy where the effect says. A unit placed on a Dark sector sits on top of that stack. |
| **Ready** | A face-up Module that can be run (5.3). |
| **Reserve** | Your own energy, beside your Company sheet (5.4). |
| **Reveal** | Flip the top tile of a Dark sector face up (8.5). |
| **Row** | Every sector on one straight line through the Field, in any of the three directions, across gaps (3.6, 3.8). |

| **Scrap** | Destroy one of your Mecha to gain energy equal to its Strength (4.2). |

| **Swap** | Two units exchange sectors, each with its tokens. |
| **Targeted** | The one adjacent sector an arrow points to, and the unit in it (3.6, 3.8). |
| **Teleport** | Pick a unit up and put it, with its tokens, onto any Dark or Cleared sector. |
| **Trophy pile** | Your collected Power Nodes and destroyed Omega units (8.2, 10.1). |
| **Uncovered** | A face-up tile that becomes the top tile without being revealed (3.4). |
| **Wreck** | A destroyed unit waiting for its Destroyed effect, marked with a Wreck marker. No effect can choose it (4.8). |

### B. Terminology notes

- **Red tokens** are **Fuse tokens**. They go off in step 1 of Overload End, which needs no name of its own. Proxy mine is now **Tick**: a charge that clamps onto a hull and blows at Overload End.
- **Difficulty:** "Difficulty variants A–D" are now **Threat columns**. The number you play sets the **Threat Level**: 1 Patrol, 2 Assault, 3 Siege, 4 Annihilation. The old text called level 4 both "insane" and "impossible".
- **New terms I added** for clarity: Occupied sector, hit, Wreck, uncovered, Exposure, Crippled, Company Trait.
- "Command" now names the phase, the Mecha action and the tokens. It reads well, but if playtesters mix them up, "Order tokens" would be the easy fix.

### C. Medium fixes made automatically

1. **Effects that activate other units** ("Drain all Gyros", "Activate all Mecha") now place Alert tokens (11.1 #6). The old rules did not say whether these resolve on the spot. Alert tokens match your Overload rule, "if a Gyro or Prototype is triggered, put an activation token on them".
2. **Wreck marker:** a Wreck carries a Wreck marker beside its Alert, so you can tell it from a Gyro alerted to Drain.
3. **Bury removes Alert tokens too.** This makes F\_10 ("Bury one Sentinel") a real counter during an Overload. Bury with no tile beneath does nothing.
4. **Overhaul:** step 5 now also removes Alert tokens and can't pick Wrecks. Step 3 refills each colour *up to* the starting amount and never reduces it.
5. **Energy on Modules:** only on ready Modules, and only of the Module's own colour when you choose where it goes; it pays only for that Module, and only in matching colours (5.3). Before, energy could be parked on exhausted Modules.
6. **Objective check** (8.2): you check the objective only before Command phase actions, so an Overload already under way always finishes. Leftover Alert tokens are simply cleared at Mission End. The earlier draft's Mop-up step is gone.
7. **Veteran campaigns reset Overhaul slots** (7.1, 10.3). "Don't erase Company sheets" would otherwise keep used Overhaul slots forever.
8. **Flares hold no tokens**, and Boosting one is skipped without damaging your Flagship (6.2).
9. **One Barrier per unit**, as S\_23 and P\_26 already assume.
10. **Sentinels revealed mid-Overload stay quiet.** This was implied by your original wording; now it is explicit (4.5, 9.2).
11. **Actions not allowed in an Overload** are listed: Attack, Scrap, Reveal, Drain, Overhaul.
12. **No legal actions** is now a loss, and you still score (8.1, 10.3). The old text only said "go to game end".
13. **Alternative routes:** playing 2x skips 2y (7.2).
14. **Orders** can be issued any number of times while you can pay (5.6).
15. **Locked upgrades** can be unlocked and filled in the same Mission End (10.2).
16. **Bio-shields:** you choose which Shield tokens damage removes first (6.3).
17. **"Deal damage" with no number means 1**, and **Destroy** is defined as not damage (6.1, 11.2).
